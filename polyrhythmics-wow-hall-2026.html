Polyrhythmics Bring 16 Years of Cinematic Funk to the WOW Hall

Seattle's seven-piece instrumental groove machine plays the WOW Hall in Eugene on Thursday, November 19, bringing 16 years of deep grooves, Afrobeat roots and big horns.

By Paolos Kidanemariam Music Journalist · October 26, 2026 · 3 min read

Some bands need a singer to tell you how to feel. Polyrhythmics let the groove do the talking. Since 2010, the Seattle band has built its sound on deep funk, Afrobeat roots, psychedelic soul and big, cinematic horn lines. As guitarist Ben Bloom puts it, the idea has always been to make the groove the focal point.

From Fela to "cinematic psych funk"

Bloom and drummer Grant Schroff started the band with Fela Kuti and Antibalas as touchstones. Sixteen years later, the sound has grown into what they call "cinematic psych funk." The Georgia Straight described it as what Massive Attack would sound like "doing bong hits with Miles Davis."

Their latest album, Life From Below (2025), runs from old-school funk on "Chophouse" to the dramatic title track. In May 2026 they released "Got to Be for Something," a gospel-soul single with singer Adryon de León of Orgōne. It is a preview of a new full-length album due in early 2027.

The band

Ben Bloom on guitar, Grant Schroff on drums, Nathan Spicer on keys, Jason Gray on bass, and a three-piece horn section: Scott Morning on trumpet, Elijah Clark on trombone and Art Brown on saxophone and flute.

Why you should go

Polyrhythmics shows are dance parties, and the band treats the crowd as part of the music, a "two-way street," in Bloom's words. The WOW Hall is the perfect room for it: close to the stage, with a wooden floor made for dancing.
