Polyrhythmics Bring the Warmth to the WOW Hall

On a cold November night in Eugene, Seattle's six-piece groove band gave the room what it needed: warmth, freedom and a reason to dance.

By Paolos Kidanemariam Music Journalist · November 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Thursday night at the WOW Hall was a fresh breath of air in a time of lack of air. In a season of coldness, in a time when a lot of us need warmth, Polyrhythmics brought it.

The music did what this band does best. It made you want to be free, be yourself and just dance. If you weren't dancing, you were bopping your head back and forth until your neck hurt. Good feeling, good vibration, from the first groove to the last.

Tight, on the one

All night the band was tight and on point, falling right on the one. Six players locked together the way only a band with 16 years behind it can. Shades on, cool as cats in the alley, they made the hardest grooves look easy, and the WOW Hall floor answered back.

Electric on stage, warm off it

Guitarist Ben Bloom was as warm off stage as he was electrifying on it. After the set he came out onto the floor to mingle with the crowd, meet people and take time with his fans. Light in spirit and genuinely kind, he clearly appreciates the people who come out to hear this music. It's the same openness he showed when he talked with us before the show, when he told Eugene to "come ready to receive, and to be part of the magic." The crowd did, and so did the band.

The verdict

The whole night was good feeling, good vibration and warmth. I loved it. Thank you, Polyrhythmics, and thank you to the WOW Hall for bringing them to Eugene. Come back soon.

The Show

  • Who: Polyrhythmics, Seattle
  • When: Thursday, November 19, 2026
  • Where: WOW Hall, 291 W 8th Ave, Eugene, Oregon
  • The band: Ben Bloom (guitars), Grant Schroff (drums), Nathan Spicer (keys), Jason Gray (bass), Scott Morning (trumpet), Art Brown (sax and flute)
