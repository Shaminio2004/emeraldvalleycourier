Five Rising Jamaican Voices to Know Right Now

Khalia, Ikaya, Armanii, Conkarah and Eesah: the new generation carrying roots, lovers rock and dancehall forward, with one video each to get you started.

By Paolos Kidanemariam Music Journalist · October 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Tap an artist to jump to their story. Images: stills from each artist's official videos.

Every generation of reggae has its torchbearers. Bob, Peter and Bunny handed it to Dennis Brown and Garnett Silk, then to Buju, Sizzla and Capleton, and then to Chronixx, Koffee and Protoje. Here are five Black Jamaican artists, born on the island and making music for the world, who are carrying it into the next chapter. Keep your ears on them.

Khalia

From Jamaica to London and back. Khalia was born in Jamaica and raised in the UK. She started recording at 15 and signed with producer Tony "CD" Kelly in 2017. Her 2020 single "Easy" put her on the map.

Her 2023 debut EP, Stay True (Ineffable Records), brought in Shaggy, Tanya Stephens, Mortimer and Blvk H3ro, a guest list most new artists only dream of. Since then she has played Rototom Sunsplash, Germany's Summerjam and Protoje's Lost In Time festival. Late in 2025 she released "Miracle" with Kingston production duo Natural High Music, the team behind records for Chronixx and Lila Iké.

Start with: "Easy," "Taboo," and "No Answer" with Tanya Stephens.

Watch: "Taboo" (official music video)

Ikaya

A Waterhouse voice. Ikaya comes from Waterhouse in Kingston, the same community that gave reggae King Jammy and Black Uhuru. She has been a steady presence among Jamaica's female vocalists for years, singing both reggae and dancehall, with songs like "Good Up," "Hard Way" and "These Tears."

2025 was a big year for her. "Walking Miracle," produced by ZJ Liquid, became a testimony song for her fans, and "Half A Man" with Sponge Music showed her tougher side.

Start with: "Walking Miracle," then "Half A Man."

Watch: "Half A Man" (official music video)

Armanii

Kingston's new heartthrob. Armanii was born and raised in Kingston and moves easily between dancehall, soulful ballads and Afropop. Billboard named him its first Caribbean Rookie of the Month. His debut album, The Impact, landed at No. 8 on Billboard's list of the best Caribbean albums of 2025.

He picked up a Best New Artist nomination at the 2025 Caribbean Music Awards and delivered a standout set at Reggae Sumfest. He has also worked with Jada Kingdom ("Balcony") and Malie Donn ("8:00PM").

Start with: "Envy," "Balcony" and "8:00PM."

Watch: "Envy" (official music video)

Conkarah

The Kingston singer who took reggae to the top of the Dutch charts. Conkarah, born Nicholas Murray, first built a following online with warm reggae covers of pop and R&B hits, alongside his own songs.

Then came "Banana" featuring Shaggy, his 2019 reggae take on Harry Belafonte's "Day-O." A TikTok dance challenge sent it around the world in 2020, and it spent five weeks at No. 1 on the Dutch Top 40.

Start with: "Banana" (feat. Shaggy), then his covers.

Listen: "Without Your Love" (feat. Sick Wit It Crew)

Eesah

Roots from behind the mixing desk. Eesah, born Ackime Nathaniel Nephew in Kingston, started out as a sound engineer at Jah Ova Evil Studio before stepping up to the mic. David Rodigan called his early song "Will Power" one of the most refreshing new songs out of Jamaica. Chronixx put him on his 2016 mixtape Roots and Chalice with "Perfect Tree."

His albums Timeless (2022) and Success (2025) came out on the Swiss label Evidence Music. Success pairs him with European producers like Little Lion Sound, plus a guest spot from UK veteran Brother Culture on "Badda We Badda."

Start with: "Mighty Warrior," "Hold A Vibe" and "Badda We Badda."

Listen: "Mighty Warrior" (official audio)

The Playlist

  • Khalia: "Easy," "Taboo," "No Answer" (with Tanya Stephens)
  • Ikaya: "Walking Miracle," "Half A Man"
  • Armanii: "Envy," "Balcony" (with Jada Kingdom)
  • Conkarah: "Banana" (feat. Shaggy)
  • Eesah: "Mighty Warrior," "Badda We Badda"
