World Music Monthly, November 2026: Kirubel Assefa, Luedji Luna, Grupo Niche and Black Sherif

An Ethio-jazz veteran with a new album on the way, a Bahian singer closing out her trilogy, Colombia's salsa institution back in the Latin GRAMMY race, and Ghana's Black Sherif in a softer, freer mode. Four artists to know this month.

By Emerald Valley Courier World Music Monthly · November 1, 2026 · 6 min read

Welcome to the second edition of World Music Monthly, our guide to the artists moving music forward far beyond the usual charts. Each month we pick four names: one from Ethiopia, one from Brazil, one from elsewhere in Latin America and one from the Afrobeats world. With the Latin GRAMMY Awards set for November 12 in Las Vegas, two of this month's picks have a stake in the night.

Ethiopia: Kirubel Assefa

Kirubel Assefa was born and raised in Addis Ababa, where he started out as a drummer in his school band. He later moved to the United States, played in reggae, Cuban, funk and world music groups, switched to bass and worked his way through the Bay Area and Los Angeles scenes. Along the way he has played with some of the biggest names in Ethiopian music, including Aster Aweke, Mahmoud Ahmed and Gigi, as well as Nigerian reggae star Majek Fashek. He was the main composer for the Los Angeles band Lasta Sound, and he has scored music for the IMAX documentary Mystery of the Nile.

His new album, T.A.B.A., is due November 20 on Ethio Jazz Factory. The title spells out the four modes at the heart of Ethiopian song: Tizita, Ambassel, Bati and Anchihoye. Kirubel wrote all 11 pieces and produced the record himself, setting those modes against jazz, funk, Afro-Cuban, reggae and West African rhythms, with the feel of Ethiopian music from the 1970s and '80s. He frames it as a continuation of the Ethio-jazz tradition rather than a revival.

Start with: "Tesmameye," from his 2017 album Dimtsepia

Listen: "Tesmameye"

Brazil: Luedji Luna

Luedji Luna (born Luedji Gomes Santa Rita in Salvador, Bahia, in 1987) grew up in a family active in Brazil's Black movement, and that sense of art as identity runs through her work. She studied at the Escola Baiana de Canto Popular and debuted in 2017 with Um Corpo no Mundo. Her music moves between MPB, jazz, neo-soul and R&B, with lyrics centered on love, desire and the African diaspora.

She is wrapping up what she calls a maritime trilogy. Bom Mesmo É Estar Debaixo D'Água opened it, and in 2025 she released Um Mar Pra Cada Um, which won the Latin GRAMMY for Best MPB Album, followed less than a month later by the surprise album Antes Que A Terra Acabe, with guests including Seu Jorge, Arthur Verocai and Milton Nascimento. That second record is now nominated for Best Portuguese Language Contemporary Pop Album at this month's Latin GRAMMYs. In May she also released Acústico Luedji Luna, mixing six new songs with stripped-down versions of older ones, and this fall her trilogy-closing tour has played Fortaleza, São Luís, Belém and Recife.

Start with: "Apocalipse," featuring Seu Jorge and Arthur Verocai

Watch: "Iôiô" (visualizer)

Latin America: Grupo Niche

Few salsa bands carry the weight of Grupo Niche. Jairo Varela, a songwriter from Quibdó in Colombia's Pacific region, founded the group in Bogotá in 1978, and it has been based in Cali since 1982. Early hits like "Buenaventura y Caney" and the anthem "Cali Pachanguero" made them one of Colombia's top salsa acts, and they have been known ever since for both hard-driving dance tunes and slow romantic numbers like "Una Aventura." Varela died in 2012, but the band kept going and kept winning, taking a GRAMMY for 40 and a Latin GRAMMY for Niche Sinfónico.

Their new album, Más Que Palabras, released this spring, is their first set of new songs in more than five years. Musical director José Aguirre wrote all nine tracks and produced the record with Yanila Varela, Jairo's daughter. It mixes classic salsa with Colombian Pacific and Atlantic coast sounds, mambo, Cuban tres and orchestral strings recorded by the Budapest Scoring Orchestra. It is nominated for Best Salsa Album, and "Realidad-Es" is up for Best Tropical Song. The band played South Florida on October 31 and has announced symphonic shows in Bogotá and Medellín for April 2027.

Start with: "Realidad-Es"

Watch: "Realidad-Es"

Afrobeats: Black Sherif

Black Sherif (born Mohammed Ismail Sherif in Konongo, in Ghana's Ashanti Region, in 2002) is one of the most distinctive voices in West African music. He first broke out with "First Sermon" in 2021, then "Kwaku the Traveller" topped Apple Music charts in both Ghana and Nigeria in 2022. His sound fuses highlife, reggae and hip-hop with drill, and he raps and sings largely in Twi. His debut album, The Villain I Never Was, arrived in 2022, he was named Artist of the Year at the 2023 Vodafone Ghana Music Awards, and his second album, Iron Boy, followed in 2025.

In early September he released Sun Sherif, a six-track EP billed as Vol. 1. It moves away from the drill edge of his albums toward a more confessional, R&B-leaning sound, with highlife touches and lyrics in Twi, English and Pidgin. He has said the project captures the music he wanted to make when he started out. "Forever" brings in rising Ghanaian singer AratheJay, and "Love Again" features Trinidad and Tobago duo Full Blown.

Start with: "Forever," featuring AratheJay

Watch: "Kwaku the Traveller"

Quick Facts

  • Grupo Niche (Colombia): salsa · Start with "Realidad-Es"
  • Black Sherif (Ghana): Afrobeats / highlife / drill · Start with "Forever"
