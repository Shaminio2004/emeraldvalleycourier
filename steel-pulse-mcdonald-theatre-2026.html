Steel Pulse Sets the McDonald Theatre on Fire

Backstage and onstage with David Hinds and Steel Pulse on the Reggae Against Racism World Tour 2026, Eugene, Oregon, October 8.

By Paolos Kidanemariam Music Journalist · October 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Photo gallery: tap any picture to enlarge. Photos: Paolos Kidanemariam / Emerald Valley Courier.

The last time I met David Hinds, it was in Hawaii. This time it was backstage at the McDonald Theatre in Eugene, minutes before Steel Pulse took the stage on the Reggae Against Racism World Tour 2026, and the years seemed to have only made him stronger. He was still standing tall, like the ancient stone pillars of Axum in Ethiopia. The signature glasses were still on. His locks now reach down his back.

A legend who takes the picture himself

He walks gracefully, and he is very kind. I asked if I could take a picture with him, only minutes before he was due on stage. He could have brushed me off. Instead, he took my phone, stepped into the frame and snapped the picture himself. Then he asked me if I was from Ethiopia. He spoke with a kindness and respect you don't normally see in a living legend.

Then I watched him warm up, quick and focused, like a soccer player getting ready for a match. And then it was on.

The whole room sang

The crowd screamed like they had been waiting in anticipation for days. David Hinds walked up to the mic, and the whole place started singing with him. From the first song, the McDonald Theatre was on fire with the good vibes of this legendary band.

He walked up to the mic, and the whole place started singing with him.

A view from the stage

I had the privilege of being on stage in the middle of the show. From there, you could feel how alive the band was. The bass player was a show of his own: he walked around the stage between the other players, then back to his spot, then over next to Mr. Hinds, holding the whole groove together the entire time.

Island warm-up

Credit also goes to special guest Jordan T, the Hawaii-based reggae artist known for his island reggae fusion sound. The opening set warmed up the fans so well that by the time Steel Pulse came on, the roof was already on fire.

Why it still matters

Steel Pulse formed in 1975 in Handsworth, Birmingham, England, with David Hinds on lead vocals and guitar from the very beginning. Their 1978 debut, Handsworth Revolution, helped shape roots reggae outside Jamaica, and with Babylon the Bandit they became the first non-Jamaican act to win the Grammy for Best Reggae Album. Nearly fifty years on, a tour called Reggae Against Racism shows the message hasn't changed, and in Eugene, neither has the fire.

Quick Facts

  • Band: Steel Pulse (Birmingham, England, est. 1975)
  • Tour: Reggae Against Racism World Tour 2026
  • Date: October 8, 2026
  • Venue: McDonald Theatre, Eugene, Oregon
  • Special guest: Jordan T (Hawaii)
  • Presented by: Concert King

The verdict: Steel Pulse is still one of the great live bands in reggae, and David Hinds is still the kind of legend who makes time for the people in front of him. If this tour comes anywhere near you, go.
