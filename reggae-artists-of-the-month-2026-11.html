Five Reggae Artists to Hear This Month

Led by this month's highlight, Mystic Marley, plus Perfect Giddimani, Blvk H3ro, Ras-I and Da'Country: new reggae from the next Marley voice, a St. Ann veteran in top form and three more artists with music out in 2026.

By Paolos Kidanemariam Music Journalist · November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Tap an artist to jump to their story. Images: stills and artwork from each artist's official YouTube uploads.

This month's picks run from a veteran who has been writing reggae hits for more than two decades to a Montego Bay singer with his first big album. What ties them together: real roots in the music, and something new to listen to right now.

This Month's Highlight

Mystic Marley

The next big Marley voice. Mystic Marley is the daughter of Stephen Marley and the granddaughter of Bob Marley. She grew up between Miami and Los Angeles, touring with her father, opening his shows and performing at Kaya Fest. Her version of her grandfather's "Misty Morning" appeared on the soundtrack album inspired by the film Bob Marley: One Love, which Stephen executive-produced.

Her new single, "In Love," came out October 30. Produced by Yo Christon and co-written with Ayaana Nash, it blends her Jamaican roots with R&B, pop and hip hop.

Start with: "In Love," then "Misty Morning."

Watch: “In Love” (official video)

Perfect Giddimani

The hand cart man from St. Ann. Perfect Giddimani was born Greg Rose in 1980 and grew up in Bamboo, a community in the parish of St. Ann, Jamaica. He first recorded as Little Ninja before settling on Perfect. His breakthrough, "Hand Cart Bwoy," entered the Jamaican charts in 2004 and stirred up a wave of support for the peddlers who sell their goods in the markets and on the streets.

In late 2009 he founded his own label, Giddimani Records, and he has kept up a steady stream of albums ever since, including Live My Life Again, which reached the top of Billboard's reggae chart. 2026 has been one of his busiest years: "Red & Dread" in February, "Yardie Mechanic" in March, Reggae 101 with producer Sinky Beatz in April (released with its own dub version), "Mother Irene" in May, a 25th anniversary edition of "Hand Cart Bwoy" in July and "Win Or Lose" in August.

Start with: "Hand Cart Bwoy," then Reggae 101 and its dub.

Watch: “Hand Cart Bwoy” (lyric video)

Blvk H3ro

Carrying the Wailers' torch. Blvk H3ro (pronounced "Black Hero") is a Jamaican singer known for blending modern reggae with other sounds. He was a 2023 GRAMMY nominee for Best Global Music Performance and has worked with Skillibeng, Stonebwoy and the late Bunny Wailer, with whom he had a direct relationship.

That link is at the heart of his newest release. He takes the lead on "Fig Tree," a new take on the Bunny Wailer song, sung with Bunny's daughter Cen'C Love. It is the first single from Rastaman 50, a VP Records album by The Wailers Trio, released November 6. Recorded in Trench Town, the project marks 50 years since three 1976 classics: Bunny Wailer's Blackheart Man, Peter Tosh's Legalize It and Bob Marley's Rastaman Vibration.

Start with: "Fig Tree," then "Live In Love."

Watch: “Fig Tree” (official music video)

Ras-I

Live roots with a modern heart. Ras-I won Song of the Year at the 2023 JaRIA awards for "Somewhere Wonderful," which the Jamaica Tourist Board later picked as an official song, and he was named Best New Reggae Artist at the 2024 Caribbean Music Awards.

His third album, Heart of Love, came out May 15 through Ineffable Records and Koastal Kings. Its 13 tracks bring in guests including Kabaka Pyramid, Govana, Khalia and Nesta, with production from Keneil Jones, JLL, the Alpha family and Ras-I himself. The lead single, "Reggae Mountain (Feel No Way)," was cut with live instruments, his answer to anyone who says reggae has lost its power.

Start with: "Heart of Love," then "These Are the Days" with Kabaka Pyramid.

Listen: “Heart of Love” (official audio)

Da'Country

Tallawah from MoBay. Da'Country is an up-and-coming reggae artist based in Montego Bay. His new album, TALLAWAH, came out September 27 on Country Pain Records. The title is a Jamaican word for strong, resilient and not to be underestimated.

The 16 tracks deal with upliftment, perseverance and daily life, and he describes the sound as roots reggae with a modern edge. Fans have already picked "SWSB" and "Good Morning" as favorites, and the album also includes "Hot," "Run That," "Dark Days" and "Solid Heart."

Start with: "Good Morning" and "SWSB." There's no official music video yet, so here is the official audio.

Listen: “Good Morning” (official audio)

Bonus pick: Al Pancho

From St. Mary fisherman to Kingston Wall Music. Al Pancho, born Owen Ricardo Brown in Kingston in 1974, grew up with his grandmother in St. Mary and worked as a fisherman from the age of seven. He started singing in church at 12, moved to Kingston alone at 14, and at 17 was discovered by the producer Bunny "Striker" Lee, who released his first singles.

Since then he has worked with Coxsone Dodd, Damian and Stephen Marley, Luciano, Cornell Campbell, Lutan Fyah and Alborosie. "On a Mission" became a No. 1 hit in Mexico, "Fuego" with Quique Neira topped the charts in Chile, and he runs his own label, Kingston Wall Music. He has been busy in 2026, with the albums Sweat Off Mi Blood, Outside We Deh and Blaze in Tha Bay and the EP Trench Town.

Start with: "On a Mission," "Unity Is Strength" with Luciano, then the Trench Town EP.

Disclosure: Al Pancho appears on songs by Paolos TK, the stage name of EVC publisher Paolos Kidanemariam, including "Bella" and "One for Me."

Watch: “Poverty” (official video)
